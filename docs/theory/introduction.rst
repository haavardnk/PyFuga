Introduction
============

Motivation and background
-------------------------

Wind turbines operating in wind farms are strongly affected by the wakes
generated by upstream machines. These wakes contain velocity deficits,
increased turbulence levels, and characteristic patterns of shear and
vorticity that influence both power production and fatigue loading.
Accurately predicting wake development is therefore essential for wind-farm
design, control, and optimisation.

High-fidelity computational fluid dynamics (CFD) methods, such as large-eddy
simulation, can reproduce detailed wake behaviour but remain too expensive
for routine engineering use, especially for layout optimisation or
control-oriented studies that require thousands of evaluations. At the
opposite end of the modelling spectrum, engineering wake models are very
fast but rely on simplified parameterisations that may not capture the full
physics of wake interactions, particularly in non-uniform or complex inflow
conditions.

Fuga was developed to bridge this gap: it provides a *linearised CFD*
approach in the frequency domain, retaining essential flow physics while
remaining computationally efficient. Fuga solves the linearised Reynolds-averaged Navier-Stokes 
(RANS) equations about a horizontally homogeneous background state, allowing the wake of a single 
turbine or an array of turbines to be computed rapidly in spectral space. The model has been used 
for more than a decade in research and industry and now forms a key component of the wake-modelling 
capabilities within PyWake.

Scope and assumptions
---------------------

Fuga is derived within a linearised RANS framework. The mean velocity
components :math:`U_i` and turbulent fluxes :math:`u_i' u_j'` are decomposed
into a background state and small perturbations induced by turbine forcing.
The background flow is assumed to be horizontally homogeneous, stationary,
and described by a specified mean velocity :math:`U(z)`, eddy viscosity
:math:`K(z)`, and density :math:`\rho`. The perturbations satisfy the
linearised momentum and continuity equations in which all nonlinear terms in
the perturbations are neglected.

The model is intended for regimes where:

- the wake is a *small perturbation* to the mean background flow,
- the inflow varies primarily with height,
- the turbulence can be represented by an eddy-viscosity closure,
- spectral methods in the horizontal directions are appropriate.

Turbines are represented using an actuator-disc forcing formulation
(:math:`f_i`), typically applied as a distributed body force aligned with the
rotor plane. The model can be evaluated for a single turbine, a row or
cluster of turbines, or full wind-farm layouts using linear superposition of
the Fourier-space solution fields.

The present documentation covers both the classical (unyawed) formulation
and the extended version allowing yaw misalignment through the curvilinear
coordinate approach, which introduces lateral velocity components and
secondary steering effects into the linearised system.

PyFuga computes look-up tables for a single, unyawed turbine only. Yaw-induced wake deflection is 
handled in PyWake. For implementation details, see PyWake's `Fuga Deflection`_ documentation.

Structure of the documentation
------------------------------

The remainder of the theory documentation is organised as follows:

- :doc:`governing_equations`  
  Presents the governing RANS equations, background-state quantities,
  and the formulation of the turbine forcing.

- :doc:`linearisation`  
  Describes the linearisation of the RANS equations about a
  horizontally homogeneous base flow, introducing the perturbation
  quantities and the assumptions underlying the linear model.

- :doc:`mixed_spectral`  
  Derives the mixed spectral formulation, where the horizontal
  directions are treated in Fourier space and the vertical direction in
  physical space. This yields the set of coupled ordinary differential
  equations that form the core of Fuga.

- :doc:`numerics`  
  Outlines the numerical procedure used to solve the mixed-spectral
  system, including the construction of the spectral matrices, solution
  of the linear system, and generation of lookup tables.

- :doc:`yawed_extension`  
  Summarises the extension of Fuga to yaw-misaligned turbines using a
  curvilinear coordinate transformation.

Supporting material includes:

- :doc:`notation` — definitions of all symbols used,  
- :doc:`references` — complete bibliography of the Fuga literature,  
- :doc:`appendices` — derivations and supplementary formulae
  corresponding to the original report.
